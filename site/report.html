Bucket boundaries

Promoter = 5★; Passive = 4★; Detractor = 1–3★. NPS = %Promoters − %Detractors, computed at full precision and rounded to a whole number. Five stars is the clearest written expression of enthusiasm; four-star reviews frequently carry reservations, mirroring the survey's 7–8 "passive" band; 1–3 stars signal dissatisfaction or materially qualified experience. These thresholds are judgment calls, so a sensitivity test follows. This is a Review-Derived NPS, not an official survey NPS.

Four products are plain flake finishing salts; Redmond Real Salt sells no flake salt, so its fine-grain everyday salt is used. Its loyalty reflects a different use case (daily cooking salt) and package size, which limits direct comparison.

Selection bias

Online reviews overrepresent customers with unusually positive or negative experiences, creating a potentially bimodal distribution that exaggerates apparent customer polarization relative to the broader customer base.

  • Review-derived NPS is not a survey of all purchasers; reviewers self-select (voluntary response bias).
  • Possible fake or incentivized reviews (including Vine) are not removed; they are disclosed where flagged.
  • The Woot endpoint returns at most 100 reviews per star-filter x sort combination; where a star bucket hits that cap, that bucket is under-collected. If capped buckets are mainly 5-star (typical for popular products) NPS is biased downward; if 1-3 star buckets are capped, upward. Cap hits are listed in the collection log.
  • Written reviews only: star-only ratings (often the large majority of Amazon ratings) are not included.
  • Product-level, not company-wide, loyalty: one SKU per brand.
  • Review volume is not sales volume.
  • Small differences in NPS (within the 95% intervals shown) should not be read as meaningful loyalty differences.

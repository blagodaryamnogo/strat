Bucket boundaries

Promoter = 5★; Passive = 4★; Detractor = 1–3★. NPS = %Promoters − %Detractors, computed at full precision and rounded to a whole number. Five stars is the clearest written expression of enthusiasm; four-star reviews frequently carry reservations, mirroring the survey's 7–8 "passive" band; 1–3 stars signal dissatisfaction or materially qualified experience. These thresholds are judgment calls, so a sensitivity test follows. This is a Review-Derived NPS, not an official survey NPS.

Selection bias

Online reviews overrepresent customers with unusually positive or negative experiences, creating a potentially bimodal distribution that exaggerates apparent customer polarization relative to the broader customer base.

  • Review-derived NPS is not a survey of all purchasers; reviewers self-select (voluntary response bias).
  • Possible fake or incentivized reviews (including Vine) are not removed; they are disclosed where flagged.
  • The Woot endpoint returns at most 100 reviews per star-filter x sort combination (about 110-125 distinct reviews per star after combining four sort orders). Where several star buckets hit that cap, the retrieved star mix is set by the cap, not by customers, so that product's NPS is shown for transparency but is NOT ranked or used in the wedge. Where only the 5-star bucket is capped, the NPS is a lower bound. Per-product cap hits are listed in the tables; raw counts are in data/audits/collection_log.json.
  • Written reviews only: star-only ratings (often the large majority of Amazon ratings) are not included.
  • Product-level, not company-wide, loyalty: one SKU per brand.
  • Review volume is not sales volume.
  • Small differences in NPS (within the 95% intervals shown) should not be read as meaningful loyalty differences.

3. Where Should Silk Road Salts Compete?

X-axis: Distinct written reviews retrieved and scored (NOT total Amazon ratings; marketplace totals could not be retrieved).

1. Competitive overview

3 of 5 competitors have comparable scores. Baseline NPS ranges from +48 (SaltWorks, n=40) to +83 (The Spice Lab, n=41). Below the 50-review target: The Spice Lab (n=41); SaltWorks (n=40) (no comparable alternative ASIN found). Lower bound only (5★ bucket capped): Jacobsen Salt Co. (+49).

Not comparable: Maldon Salt (sample NPS -31, n=511; capped buckets: 5/4/3/1★); Redmond Real Salt (sample NPS -32, n=497; capped buckets: 5/4/1★). The endpoint returns at most ~110–125 distinct reviews per star bucket, so for these heavily reviewed products the retrieved star mix is set by the cap, not by customers. Their sample scores are an artifact of retrieval and say nothing about loyalty; they are excluded from ranks and from the wedge test.

2. Entry wedge

No high-volume / low-loyalty wedge can be shown from this data. Among the comparable products, none with at-or-above-median retrieved volume has below-median NPS. Maldon Salt, Redmond Real Salt could not be tested because of capped samples. The entry case therefore rests on the dissatisfaction themes below, not on a measured weak incumbent.

4. Positioning, target customer & validation (proposals, not evidence)

Target customer (assumption): home cooks who already buy premium finishing salt and gift food items. Positioning (proposal): globally inspired, botanically infused finishing salts with documented provenance in resealable rigid packaging, addressing the measured complaint themes above. Prices and features are not yet tested.

  1. Smoke test: landing page with 3 SKUs and a gift set; measure email sign-up rate from ~$200 of targeted ads.
  2. Packaging test: mail 20 home cooks a pouch vs. a resealable tin; survey clumping/spillage after 4 weeks.
  3. Blind taste panel: 15–20 participants compare an infused prototype vs. a leading competitor; ask a true NPS question.

Key uncertainties: retrieval caps, one SKU per brand, review volume ≠ sales, keyword coding misses sarcasm/negation, and no evidence yet that customers will pay a premium for infusion or provenance.

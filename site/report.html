Bucket boundaries

Promoter = 5★; Passive = 4★; Detractor = 1–3★. NPS = %Promoters − %Detractors, computed at full precision and rounded to a whole number. Five stars is the clearest written expression of enthusiasm; four-star reviews frequently carry reservations, mirroring the survey's 7–8 "passive" band; 1–3 stars signal dissatisfaction or materially qualified experience. These thresholds are judgment calls, so a sensitivity test follows. This is a Review-Derived NPS, not an official survey NPS.

Selection bias

Online reviews overrepresent customers with unusually positive or negative experiences, creating a potentially bimodal distribution that exaggerates apparent customer polarization relative to the broader customer base.

Other limitations

  • Headline scores use each listing's Amazon star histogram, hand-recorded from the live page (date and recorder stored). Histograms cover all ratings, including star-only ratings, and Amazon labels them 'global ratings', which may include other Amazon stores.
  • Amazon shows whole-number percentages, so each histogram NPS carries up to about +/-2 points of rounding error; differences of a few points are not meaningful.
  • Written-review samples (capped at ~110-125 per star by the Woot endpoint) are used only for themes and excerpts; their sample NPS is shown for reference.
  • Review-derived NPS is not a survey of all purchasers; reviewers self-select (voluntary response bias).
  • Possible fake or incentivized reviews (including Vine) are not removed; they are disclosed where flagged.
  • Product-level, not company-wide, loyalty: one SKU per brand.
  • Review volume is not sales volume.

3. Where Should Silk Road Salts Compete?

1. Competitive overview

5 of 5 competitors have comparable scores from each listing's Amazon star histogram (hand-recorded; ±2 pts rounding). Baseline NPS ranges from +74 (Jacobsen Salt Co., 1,275 ratings) to +88 (The Spice Lab, 63 ratings). The Spice Lab (+88), Maldon Salt (+86), Redmond Real Salt (+86) are within the combined rounding error (±2 pts each) of one another, so their order is not meaningful. Small rating base: The Spice Lab (63 ratings).

2. Entry wedge (measured)

Rule: total Amazon ratings ≥ median (1,275) and baseline NPS below median (+86.0). Qualifying: Jacobsen Salt Co. (1,275 ratings, NPS +74). Jacobsen Salt Co. qualifies on volume only by sitting exactly at the median (3 of 5 by volume, well below Maldon Salt at 47,071). Ratings count is a proxy for sales, not a measure of it, so this is a candidate wedge, not a proven one.

4. Positioning, target customer & validation (proposals, not evidence)

Target customer (assumption): home cooks who already buy premium finishing salt and gift food items. Positioning (proposal): globally inspired, botanically infused finishing salts with documented provenance in resealable rigid packaging, addressing the measured complaint themes above. Prices and features are not yet tested.

  1. Smoke test: landing page with 3 SKUs and a gift set; measure email sign-up rate from ~$200 of targeted ads.
  2. Packaging test: mail 20 home cooks a pouch vs. a resealable tin; survey clumping/spillage after 4 weeks.
  3. Blind taste panel: 15–20 participants compare an infused prototype vs. a leading competitor; ask a true NPS question.

Key uncertainties: one SKU per brand; ratings ≠ sales; keyword coding misses sarcasm/negation; no evidence yet that buyers pay more for infusion or provenance.
